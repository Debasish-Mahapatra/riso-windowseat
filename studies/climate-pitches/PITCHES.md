# Climate short: four pitches

Snowman and Rivers in the Sky were made: see `films/snowman/` and `films/rivers-in-the-sky/`.

Each pitch is a 70-second, 16:9 film with no dialogue, carried by music and a title or two. Each
comes in a different 2D style, with one test frame: `snowman.html`, `wall.html`, `scarf.html` and `rivers.html` here (all four side by side in `all-four.jpg`). The test frames are
Canvas 2D pages, and a film would be built the same way: HTML and JavaScript, every pixel drawn
in code. Any story can move to a different style.

## 1. Snowman (linocut: black and red on cream paper)

Every winter a boy builds a snowman outside his house in the hills. Every winter he is a bit
bigger and the snowman a bit smaller.

The camera stays on one house and slope for 31 winters.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–5 | Snow falls on the title. 1994. |
| 5–16 | The boy (6) and his dad roll a huge snowball. On a crate, the boy pushes in the carrot nose, and dad's hat goes on top. (This is the test frame.) |
| 16–34 | The winters flash past with a year stamp each. The boy becomes a teenager, then a man. The snowman goes chest-high, then knee-high, then grey slush on brown grass. It keeps the same hat and the same carrot, so the carrot grows and the hat swallows the snowman. One year there's a big snowfall and a big snowman, and he cheers, then it shrinks again (weather varies, the trend doesn't). The snowline on the mountains creeps up. |
| 34–50 | 2025. He is 37, with a daughter of 6 in his old red scarf. There's no snow, only rain. He scrapes the freezer and builds her a snowman the size of a cup, on a plate, under the old hat. |
| 50–64 | It melts on the windowsill, so she carries it back into the freezer to keep it. He looks at the mountains, which are nearly bare now. |
| 64–70 | Her drawing of a giant snowman is taped to the freezer door. Last line: "How small it gets is up to us." |

**Tone.** Funny, then bittersweet. Wordless, with a year stamp per winter.

## 2. Rivers in the Sky (watercolour)

One drop of water crosses South America several times without touching the sea, because the
forest keeps raining it back up. Then the forest is cut.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–8 | Sun on the Atlantic. A drop rises as vapour, and the trade winds carry it west. |
| 8–24 | The Amazon: cloud, then rain on the canopy, then up through roots and a kapok trunk and out of a leaf, then cloud again. Two loops, each one further west, and the flying river builds across the sky. (This is the test frame.) |
| 24–34 | At the Andes the wind turns south. The drop rains on a farm far away, and a girl runs out into it. |
| 34–50 | The next season: smoke, chainsaws, pasture. The drop lands on bare red ground and runs off into a brown river and back to the sea. The flying river thins, the farm's sky stays empty and the maize wilts. |
| 50–64 | The girl, grown up, plants saplings with others. The trees grow in time-lapse, a leaf breathes the drop out again, and clouds build. |
| 64–70 | Rain on the farm. "The forest makes its own rain." |

**Science.** A large share of Amazon rain is recycled forest transpiration. The low-level jet
carries it south along the Andes to the La Plata basin. Deforestation cuts transpiration and
downwind rain.

## 3. The Scarf (knitted and felt)

In 1950 a young woman starts a scarf: one row a year, in the colour of how warm that year was.
Three generations keep knitting it. In 2025 it turns out to be the warming stripes.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–6 | 1950. By a window, she casts on in deep blue yarn. |
| 6–26 | Every New Year's Eve she knits one row: the radio, her wedding, a baby, the baby growing up. The rows are blue and pale blue, and the scarf spills off her lap. |
| 26–40 | Her hands start to shake, and her daughter takes over the needles. The rows turn pale, then pink, then red. A TV arrives, then a phone. |
| 40–54 | The granddaughter knits through heatwaves: a fan, a power cut. The reds darken, and she has to buy darker wool than the shop usually stocks. |
| 54–64 | 2025. The three of them stretch the scarf across the room. The camera rises, and it's the stripes. (This is the test frame.) |
| 64–70 | The 2026 row isn't started yet, and two balls of red wool wait. "The next rows are ours to knit." |

**Data.** The colours come from the real global temperature record, one row per year, on
Ed Hawkins' warming-stripes scale. The test frame uses approximate values from memory. The film
needs the real dataset (GISTEMP or HadCRUT).

## 4. Paint It Back (Warli painting: white rice paste on a red mud wall)

A Warli mural of a village comes alive. As the forest is cut and the heat rises, the rain first
fails, then comes all at once and washes the painting off the wall. The next morning the village
paints it back, starting with trees.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0–8 | A bamboo stick paints a white circle on red mud: the sun. Figures appear. |
| 8–24 | The painting lives: the tarpa player blows, the dancers spiral, then sowing, the first rain, the rice and the harvest, with deer and birds in the forest. |
| 24–40 | Trucks and a sawmill, also drawn in Warli, arrive and the trees fall one by one. The painted sun grows bigger, the dancers slow, the well rope comes up empty and the rice withers. |
| 40–50 | A cloudburst. Real water streams down the wall, the paint runs and the figures dissolve. (The test frame shows the start of this.) |
| 50–64 | Morning. Hands repaint the wall: a tree, then many trees. The birds come back and the dance circle forms again. |
| 64–70 | "Paint It Back." |

**Science.** A warmer atmosphere holds more water, so the monsoon brings longer dry spells and
heavier bursts.

## Other 2D styles I can draw in code

- Indian folk styles:
  - Gond (animals and trees filled with dots and dashes);
  - Madhubani;
  - Pattachitra (Odisha scroll painting);
  - Kalighat.
- Ink-wash brush painting.
- Shadow puppets (Lotte Reiniger silhouettes, or Andhra leather puppets).
- Pixel art.
- Chalk on a blackboard, or pencil sketch.
- Stained glass.
- Comic-book halftone.
- A clean flat explainer style.
- Riso, this repo's house style.
- Paper cut-out.
