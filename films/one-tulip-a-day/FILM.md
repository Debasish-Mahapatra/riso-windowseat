# One Tulip a Day

Built from the approved story: 2:12 (132 s) at 1920 × 1080 (16:9). It is drawn on twos at 24 fps
in the kit's Canvas 2D engine. Riso inks are fluorescent pink, blue and yellow; darks are
overprints. The score is a waltz in F: clarinet for him, glockenspiel over soft vibraphone for
her, with pizzicato and strings, all on CC0 recordings embedded in the file (see
`AUDIO-SOURCES.md`). The delivered MP4 is `out/one-tulip-a-day.mp4`.

This remakes the 70 s first cut (`../../../one-tulip-a-day/`, HTML and MP4). It keeps the
characters, print look, waltz and title, and fixes the story's logic.

## Premise

A shy young man buys one tulip a day from a florist and never manages to say a word to her.
When she leaves town, he has to speak or lose her.

- **Bram** has round glasses, a navy mop of hair, a blue coat, a pink scarf and a bicycle
  with a front basket and a bell.
- **Fleur**, the florist, has a red bob with a pink clip, a yellow sweater and an apron. In
  winter she wears a yellow coat and a pink beret. Her name is Dutch for "flower".

## The story

**1. Title (0:00–0:06).** The title prints one ink at a time. A tiny cyclist crosses under it
and rings his bell twice.

**2. Day 1 (0:06–0:22).** A spring morning on an Amsterdam canal. Bram cycles past a flower
stall and sees Fleur setting out buckets of tulips. His bike wobbles and he brakes too hard; the
bell dings by accident. At the counter he points at a red tulip. She holds one out, their
fingers touch, and time stops: pink starburst, hearts. Both blush. He takes the tulip and rides
off wobbling, looking back. She watches him go and touches her cheek. (The coin in the draft was
cut: it added a beat without adding meaning.)

**3. Every day (0:22–0:48).** The montage has two panels and a day counter: DAY 2, 3, 5 … 270.
- *Left panel:* the hand-off at the counter, close on their hands. On four of the days he
  tries to speak; his speech bubble fills with "…" and pops.
- *Right panel:* his attic. Each evening he puts today's tulip in a jar on the sill and hangs
  yesterday's upside down to dry on strings across the ceiling. The ceiling fills with rows of
  red.
- *Seasons:* spring blossom; then summer, when the stall sells sunflowers but she brings a
  tulip out from under the counter just for him; then autumn rain, when she already has his
  tulip in her hand before he arrives; then winter and mittens.
- *What it shows:* he never notices that she likes him too. The audience does.

**4. The last tulip (0:48–1:02).** December, day 270, snow. The stall's shutter is down, with a
hand-painted sign on it: CLOSED, with a little tulip drawing. Fleur waits beside it in her coat,
with a suitcase. She gives him one last tulip with a small brown paper tag tied to the stem, and
waits. His bubble fills… and pops. Her small, sad smile. She waves and walks off with her
suitcase toward the STATION → sign. He stands in the falling snow while it gathers on his hair.

**5. The tag (1:02–1:12).** His attic, under 269 dried tulips. He sits on the bed turning the
fresh one in his fingers, and the clock ticks. He puts it in the jar and the tag flips over:
**11:05 · PLATFORM 3 · FLEUR**. That is her train, and her name, which he never knew. The
clock says 10:52. A beat, then a yellow burst. He stands up.

**6. The race (1:12–1:28).** He pulls down every string: an enormous armful of dried tulips,
with the fresh one in the middle. He bolts downstairs and jams it into the bike basket. As he
rides hard through the snowy city, the dried tulips shatter:
- petals stream behind him in a red trail;
- a bump over a canal bridge throws up a burst of them;
- at a tram crossing he rings his bell and squeezes through, scattering pigeons.

The station tower clock reads 11:04.

**7. Platform 3 (1:28–1:40).** A steam train, a locomotive and three carriages, stands at
platform 3. The conductor blows his whistle and the train starts to roll. Fleur sits at a window
in the second carriage, looking down. Bram rides onto the platform with a basket of bare stems,
one red tulip still standing in the middle. He pedals alongside her carriage but the train
gathers speed. He takes a breath and shouts. The bubble bursts open: **"FLEUR!"** It is the
first word he has ever said to her. Inside, she turns and sees him. Behind him, red petals run
the length of the platform. She reaches up and pulls the red emergency handle.

**8. Steam (1:40–1:46).** Brakes screech, the wheels spark and the train stops. Steam rolls over
the platform until everything is white. Only the hiss is left.

**9. Two words (1:46–1:58).**
1. The steam thins. Fleur has stepped down and stands on the platform with her suitcase.
2. He holds out the last tulip. A small bubble: **"Hi."** She laughs: **"Hi."**
3. She takes it; their fingers touch and time stops again, and this time she doesn't let go.
   They hug.
4. Behind them the conductor leans out, rolls his eyes, smiles and blows his whistle. The train
   leaves without her.

**10. Day 1 (1:58–2:05, end card to 2:10).** From high above, one red line of petals winds
through the white city from his attic to the station. Then sunset on the canal: they ride home,
her on the back rack, the suitcase in the basket and the tulip behind her ear. The counter
prints **DAY 1**.

## Why it holds together

| Question | Answer in the film |
|---|---|
| How can he keep 270 tulips? | He dries them on strings across his ceiling (Montage, Tag). |
| Why do the petals fly off on the ride? | Dried flowers are brittle, so the cobbles and the wind shatter them. |
| Why is exactly one tulip left? | It's the only fresh one: the last tulip she gave him. |
| How does he know where she is? | The tag: 11:05, platform 3, her name. |
| Why is "FLEUR!" his first word? | He learned her name from the tag. After 270 wordless days, the shy man shouts it. |
| How is she on the platform after the train left? | She pulled the emergency brake and stepped down. The train leaves after the hug, without her. |
| The train | Always the same train: a locomotive and three carriages, with her in the second. Nothing is added or dropped between shots. |
| The times | Tag 11:05; attic clock 10:52; station clock 11:04, then 11:05 as it pulls out. |
| Does she like him too? | Yes: she watches him leave on Day 1, saves a tulip for him in summer, waits with it in autumn, and writes the tag. |
| Why is she leaving? | The stall has closed; the shutter and the suitcase say so. No other reason is given. |
| Tulips in summer? | Out of season, which is exactly why the one she keeps under the counter means something. |

## Motifs

- **The speech bubble.** It fills with "…" and pops five times: four in the montage and one at
  the goodbye. It finally holds "FLEUR!", and then "Hi." The film's arc is him learning to
  speak.
- **The touch.** Time stops at the first hand-off, and again at the last, when she holds on.
- **The bell.** It rings under the title, by accident at the first stop, at the tram, and at
  the end.
- **The counter.** DAY 2 … DAY 270 runs through the story, then resets to DAY 1 at the end.
- **The tulip trail.** His grand gesture falls apart on the way. What reaches her is one tulip
  and one word.

## References observed

The full notes, with sources, are in `REFERENCES.md`. What each one changed:

- **NS 3737, a 4-6-0 of the 3700 class.** The locomotive is laid out on a 10-unit grid. The
  boiler line is flat, broken only by chimney, dome, valves and cab, with green and brass as its
  colours.
- **1950s carriages.** One window module repeats, with doors and steps only at the ends. The
  train is always a locomotive, tender and three carriages.
- **Noodrem (emergency brake).** A cast NOODREM plate with a stirrup handle that she yanks down.
  Its colours are not confirmed: the photos are black and white.
- **Conductor.** A dark double-breasted jacket, a 1950s peaked cap with cord and badge, a whistle,
  and the round signal disc (spiegelei) with a green centre and a white ring. The disc's colours
  are unconfirmed.
- **Amsterdam Centraal's shed.** A semicircular roof with a fine rib rhythm, and the perron named
  on a board.
- **Bloemenmarkt stall.** Stepped planks and zinc buckets. The striped awning is only confirmed
  for 2008.
- **Dutch transport bike.** The crate turns with the front wheel, and the rider sits upright.
- **Drying tulips.** A small, closed, wrinkled, darker cone on a thin stem. A fully dried tulip
  was not inspected.
- **GVB trams 465 and 533.** A short box body, dark blue below and cream above, with a rounded
  roofline and a single bow collector on the wire. Trams in 1950s service were not inspected.

## Picture notes

- **Rigs.** The cast shares one rig with anatomical IK: elbows and knees choose the physical
  bend, and hand-offs happen at chest height.
- **Conductor.** He is built on Bram's own skeleton (`class Conductor extends Him`), so he stands
  at the couple's scale:
  - at 0.95 on the platform, and at 1.4 beside the couple in the finale;
  - his cap's band sits at the brow and its peak over his eyes;
  - he raises the disc forward of his face so that its arm reads.
- **Tram.** It is drawn at true scale, about 1.75 times a cyclist's height (s = 2.9), with:
  - two bogies on grooved rails, lit windows with passengers, and doors at both platforms;
  - a navy lower body so Bram's blue coat stays readable against it;
  - its bow trailing up to the overhead wire.
- **Steam white-out.** The white is a wash under the steam billows, not a flat fill over them.
  The white keeps drifting, so it reads as steam rather than a blank frame. The finale opens on
  the same white and thins it for the reveal.
- **Shared timings.** Picture timings the score reads are named once: `MONTAGE_CONTACT`,
  `MONTAGE_BUBBLE`, `BUBBLE_FILL` and `BUBBLE_SHRINK`, `LAST_BUBBLE`, `TAG_REVEAL`, `TAG_CLOCK`
  and `CLOCK_TICK`, and `inkPasses()`.

## Sound direction

- **The waltz.** A waltz in F. His clarinet theme leads Day 1 and the montage; her
  glockenspiel theme answers.
- **Montage.** The pizzicato gets busier with each season: shaker in summer, harp in autumn,
  sleigh bells in winter.
- **The goodbye and the attic.** The goodbye turns to D minor. The attic is only the clock's
  pizzicato tick and low piano.
- **The race.** A faster ostinato climbs by key (F, G, A) as he nears the station.
- **Sync points.** Bell at the first stop; the touch; the whistle as the train starts; the shout;
  the emergency brake.
- **Silence.** The steam is the one deliberate silence, only the hiss. Her theme returns softly
  as the steam clears on "Hi."
- **Ending.** A button on DAY 1, with a last bell.
- **Samples.** Bells and the steam whistle are real CC0 recordings (BigSoundBank). Instruments
  are VSCO 2 CE and VCSL (CC0), as listed in the first cut's `SOURCES.md`.

## Score

**How it is built.** The score is `renderAudio()` in `index.html`, using the kit's `Score`, room
and mastering.
- 106 CC0 recordings are embedded as a 48 kHz Ogg Vorbis bank.
- About 2,000 sampled notes are mixed in JS into one dry and one reverb-send stereo buffer. The
  mix is sample-accurate and seeded.
- A Web Audio node per note is what made the first cut's render crawl, and Firefox cannot feed
  voices in incrementally.
- Procedural voices come from the kit, or are built on it, for sounds with no clean recording:
  - the conductor's pea whistle;
  - chuffs, rail joints, the brake's air dump, squeal and sparks;
  - steam, wings, and the tram passing;
  - paper, card, room tone, wind and rain.
- The kit's `tick`, `contact` and `paper` are renamed `sndTick`, `sndContact` and `sndPaper`,
  because the film already uses those names.
- The room and the WAV writer are window-seat's.

**His theme and hers.** His is `HIS`, C–F–G | A, played on clarinet. Hers is `HER`, A–C–F |
E–D, played on glockenspiel over vibraphone. The three title inks print her motif, A, C and F.
When FLEUR is written on the tag, her motif plays again. "FLEUR!" is her motif at full orchestra.

**Grid and keys.** Every bar length is derived from the picture:

| Passage | Bar length | Derived from | Key |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 and the montage | 1.25 s, 144 beats/min | Half a DAY panel (`MONTAGE[0][2] / 2`) | F major |
| The first touch | Fermata | The waltz resumes at `ST.everyday − 4 bars` | F |
| Goodbye | 9 bars, 1.53 s | Fitted to the shot | D minor, with A7 |
| Attic and tag | Free time | Pizzicato ticks on the clock's `CLOCK_TICK` grid | D minor |
| Idea | — | A burst back into F | F |
| Race | 12 bars, 1.0 s | Fitted to 76.5–88.5 | Climbs F → G → A |
| Platform chase | 6 bars | From `depart` to `shout` | A major |
| The shout | 2 bars | Fill to the brake | F major, a chromatic lift from E7 |
| Finale | 1.37 s, then 1.40 s, then 1.55 s | Hug → trail, trail → DAY 1, DAY 1 → credits | F |

The finale's last bar lengthens into the button: a gentle ritardando.

**Other picture-driven sounds.** Each reads the picture's own data:
- **Title:** press thumps on each ink pass.
- **Day 1:** a harp flutter when he first sees her, and the clarinet's wobble while the bike
  wobbles.
- **Montage:**
  - a glockenspiel ding on every hand-off, at `MONTAGE_CONTACT`;
  - clarinet staccato on each "…" dot, then a pop and a vibraphone "never mind";
  - a paper flick as each DAY counter turns;
  - light rain in the autumn panels.
- **Goodbye:** the pop at the goodbye, and a distant, low-passed locomotive whistle as she walks
  off.
- **Attic:** the jar's clink, the tag's flick, and the clock ticking.
- **Decide:** the rustle of the strings coming down, and footsteps on the floorboards.
- **Race:** shattering petals, the bridge bump, his double ring at the tram, pigeons' wings, the
  tram passing, and the tower clock's minute hand.
- **Platform:** the engine's whistle at `depart`. Its chuffs follow `trainD`, four per turn of
  the drivers, and the rail joints come every 420 px.
- **Finale:**
  - "Hi." on his clarinet and "Hi." on her glockenspiel, with her laugh;
  - the second touch, bigger;
  - the conductor's whistle, then the engine's whistle and chuffs as it leaves without her;
  - two press thumps as DAY 1 prints (`inkPasses(RED)`);
  - a button on the credits, and a last double ring as they ride off.

**Deliberate quiet.** Two moments hold back on purpose:
- **The steam, 103.4–106.5.** Only the hiss and room tone, about −30 LUFS short-term. Her theme
  re-enters on a held bed as the steam clears.
- **The breath before the shout, 95.25–96.0.** The band thins to a snare and timpani roll with an
  inhaled noise swell.

Otherwise no 200 ms window drops 10 dB under its surroundings: pedals carry the title → Day 1 and
attic → tag cuts.

**Measured** (Firefox):

```
I −16.1 LUFS, LRA 9.1 LU, TP −1.5 dBTP, clipped 0, firefox byte-identical (cdb66235bdab04fe)
```

- Chromium matches within 1 LSB of summation jitter.
- ffmpeg's EBU R128 reads I −16.1 LUFS, LRA 9.1 LU.
- Bands: low-mid 45%, low 31%, mid 14%, high 2.4%, air 0.16%.
- Stereo correlation is 0.90.
- The render takes 5.2 s in Firefox and about 12 s in Chromium.
- The true-peak ceiling is set to −1.5 dBTP to leave room for AAC.
- The one listed discontinuity, at 11.155 s, is the bell's strike.

**Heard: nothing.** I could not listen in this session. The claims above are meters,
spectrogram strips and the code. The strips checked were the bell, the platform, the brake and
steam, and the attic. Perceptual quality is not claimed. The parts most likely to sound
artificial:
- the synthesised pea whistle, brake squeal and chuffs;
- the contrabass pizzicato recordings, which glide in pitch after the attack;
- the balance of her glockenspiel against his clarinet.

## Decisions

1. **Format.** 16:9, 1920 × 1080 on a 1280 × 720 CSS canvas. `tools/lib/browser.mjs` and
   `shoot.mjs` were patched to capture non-square films 1:1.
2. **Length.** 2:12 (132 s).
3. **Names.** Bram and Fleur.
4. **Conductor and tram.** Both beats are kept. Each was redrawn at true scale after review.

## Verification

- **`verify.mjs`.** `seek` is pure in `t` in Chromium and Firefox across 62 frames, including
  every shot boundary. It was re-run after the conductor, tram and steam changes.
- **`review.mjs`.** No page errors and no metadata notes. All 57 review stills were read.
- **Shot sheets.** These were read for:
  - the conductor at 88.5–91 and 113–117;
  - the tram at 83.7–86.2;
  - the steam at 102.4–108.3.
  The native crops were checked for the cap and the disc arm.
- **Score.** See Score, above.

## Delivery

`out/one-tulip-a-day.mp4` is 225 MB. `out/` is git-ignored; rebuild it with:

```
node render.mjs ../films/one-tulip-a-day/index.html --fps 24 --size 1080 --engine firefox
```

- **Video.** H.264 High, 1920 × 1080, yuv420p, 24 fps, CRF 16: exactly 3,168 frames, 2:12.000.
- **Audio.** AAC-LC, 48 kHz stereo. The muxed audio reads I −16.1 LUFS, LRA 9.1 LU, peak
  −1.6 dBFS.
- **Render and decode.** It rendered in Firefox in 505 s. A full decode reports no errors.
- **Frames inspected.** Encoded frames at 15.9, 84.7, 89.3, 104, 116.4 and 127.6 s.
- **Bank rebuild.** The bank was then rebuilt without two unused recordings: the brush snare and
  the muted triangle. The score renders byte-identical in Firefox before and after
  (`cdb66235bdab04fe`), so the MP4's soundtrack matches the final `index.html`.
- **No smaller copy.** A CRF 23 copy was still 153 MB, and downscaling would add moiré to the
  screens, so only the CRF 16 master is kept.

## Remaining weaknesses and not reviewed

- **Sound.** Unheard; see Score.
- **Speed.** Motion at speed was judged from strips, not from watching the MP4 in real time.
- **Colours.** The noodrem, the spiegelei and 1950s tram colours come from black-and-white or
  modern references.
- **Scale.** The tower shot is a wide establishing shot, so the cyclist is small there on
  purpose. The street houses are a backdrop row at a smaller scale than the cast.
